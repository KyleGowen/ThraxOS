# Output format

Use the smallest format that preserves provenance and uncertainty.

## Short answer

For a narrow question, provide:

1. Answer and confidence.
2. Two to four strongest supporting facts with direct citations.
3. Applicability caveat, especially version or hardware generation.
4. Unresolved conflict or missing evidence, if any.

## Substantive investigation

Lead with a concise conclusion, then use this table:

| Claim | Finding | Best evidence | Applicability | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Atomic testable claim | Supported, contradicted, mixed, or unresolved | Direct citation and source class | Version, generation, date, and play style | High, moderate, low, or unknown |

Follow with only the relevant sections:

- **Disagreements:** Describe credible conflicting accounts and why they may differ.
- **Thraximundar applicability:** Separate findings that directly apply from analogies or unknowns.
- **Search limitations:** Note inaccessible material, missing primary sources, thin coverage, or uncertain dates.
- **Recommended next step:** Suggest a safe read-only check, further research, or the appropriate ThraxOS workflow. Do not silently perform mutations.

## Citation rules

- Cite the page that directly supports the sentence, not a search-results page.
- Prefer original sources; cite a secondary source when it contributes distinct analysis or preserves otherwise unavailable history.
- Attribute firsthand reports to their authors without exposing information beyond the public page.
- Quote sparingly and preserve meaning. Use paraphrase when a quotation is unnecessary.
- Give publication and retrieval dates when historical sequence or page drift matters.
- Label archive captures explicitly.

## Language rules

Use exact labels: official, observed in an artifact, firsthand report, community summary, inferred, disputed, or unknown. Avoid `confirmed`, `everyone agrees`, `proven`, and similar wording unless the evidence rubric genuinely supports it.

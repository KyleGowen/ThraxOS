# Evidence rubric

Grade each material claim independently. Confidence reflects the evidence available for that claim, not the reputation of an entire website.

## Source classes

| Class | Typical evidence | Default weight |
|---|---|---|
| Primary authoritative | Official manual, release note, developer repository, maintainer statement, published specification | Highest for documented behavior; still check version and date |
| Primary empirical | Reproducible measurement, diagnostic output, photograph, video, benchmark, or test procedure | High when the artifact and method are clear |
| Firsthand experience | The author directly owned, played, charted, repaired, organized, or observed the subject | Moderate; raise with detail and independent corroboration |
| Expert synthesis | A knowledgeable author explains multiple primary sources and links them | Moderate; verify important claims at the linked sources |
| Community summary | Wiki, guide, FAQ, recommendation thread, or apparent consensus | Useful for orientation; trace consequential claims upstream |
| Hearsay or speculation | Unattributed assertion, memory without evidence, repost, rumor, or guess | Lead only; do not rely on it alone |

## Confidence labels

- **High:** Direct authoritative or empirical evidence applies to the exact version or hardware, with no credible contradiction; or multiple independent strong sources converge.
- **Moderate:** Credible firsthand or expert evidence converges, but primary documentation, exact applicability, or complete testing is missing.
- **Low:** One limited source, unclear applicability, indirect evidence, unresolved conflict, or mostly hearsay.
- **Unknown:** No reliable public evidence found, or available evidence cannot answer the claim.

## Independence checks

- Determine whether several posts repeat one original claim, quote the same maintainer, or share the same screenshot.
- Treat linked copies and cross-posts as one evidence chain.
- Do not use upvotes, likes, search position, or number of replies as a proxy for truth.
- Note incentives and conflicts only when evidenced; do not speculate about motives.

## Applicability checks

Before applying a claim, compare:

- StepManiaX hardware generation, controller revision, firmware, and sensor layout;
- ITGMania, StepMania, OutFox, theme, plugin, and operating-system versions;
- pad versus keyboard, singles versus doubles, and casual versus stamina or competitive play;
- chart revision, pack edition, timing data, and difficulty scale;
- publication date versus later releases or changed community norms.

## Conflict handling

1. State the conflicting claims separately.
2. Compare source class, firsthand status, date, version, and reproducibility.
3. Look for a version change or narrower condition that reconciles them.
4. Preserve the disagreement when the evidence does not resolve it.
5. Never average incompatible claims into a false consensus.
